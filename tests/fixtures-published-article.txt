Most bad contractor stories don't start with bad work. They start with a question nobody asked.

The deposit that disappeared. The "license" that turned out to belong to somebody's cousin. The job that was supposed to take two weeks and is now on month three. Almost every one of those could have been caught with a five-minute conversation before anybody signed anything.

Here are the seven questions worth asking every contractor, every time, plus what a good answer sounds like.

# 1. "Can I see your license?"

In Tennessee, any project of $25,000 or more requires a state contractor license. In nine counties, including Davidson, Rutherford, and Robertson, jobs between $3,000 and $25,000 need a home improvement license too.

Don't just take their word for it. Look the license up on the state's search at <a href="https://search.cloud.commerce.tn.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener" style="color:#E8621A;text-decoration:underline">search.cloud.commerce.tn.gov</a>, and make sure the name on the license matches the business you're hiring. Nobody is allowed to work under someone else's license.

**Good answer:** They hand you a number without hesitating and it checks out.

# 2. "Are you insured, and can you send me the certificate?"

You want general liability insurance at a minimum. If they have even one employee, Tennessee requires workers' compensation too. Without it, an injury on your property can turn into your problem.

**Good answer:** "Sure, I'll have my agent email it to you today."

# 3. "Who's actually doing the work?"

The person who gives you the estimate isn't always the person who shows up. Ask whether it's their own crew or subcontractors, and who's in charge on site day to day.

**Good answer:** A straight answer with names, not "we'll figure that out."

# 4. "What's the deposit, and what's the payment schedule?"

Tennessee's licensing board advises keeping a down payment to no more than one-third of the total contract, and never paying the full amount before the work is done. Payments should be tied to finished stages of the job, not dates on a calendar.

And never pay in cash or sign over an insurance check.

**Good answer:** A reasonable deposit, then payments as milestones are completed.

# 5. "Will you pull the permits?"

Permits and inspections protect you. They're also the contractor's job, not yours. A contractor who asks you to pull the permit yourself, or says you "don't really need one," is telling you something.

**Good answer:** "Yes, permits are included in the price."

# 6. "Can I get all of this in writing?"

Scope of work, materials, price, payment schedule, start and finish dates, and warranty. If it isn't written down, it didn't happen.

**Good answer:** A written contract before any work starts, without you having to push for it.

# 7. "Can I talk to a couple of recent customers?"

Not the ones from five years ago. Jobs from the last few months, ideally similar to yours. A real contractor has people who'll pick up the phone and vouch for them.

**Good answer:** Two or three names and numbers, offered without a fight.
